# Core User Journey

Millie's English class is reading John Steinbeck's *East of Eden* over four weeks and will write essays at the end. ThreadTracker starts empty, so Millie, her classmates, and her teacher each register an account. Her teacher creates a chart for the book, with the chapters as rows and the literary elements such as "free will," "sibling rivalry," and "Cain and Abel" as columns, and adds Millie and her classmates as members (0). Millie moves her tracker to the last chapter, so the chart shows every row up to that chapter and hides every row after that chapter (1). In Chapter 3, Millie notes that Charles turns on Adam after their father rejects his gift, so she opens the cell for Chapter 3 and "sibling rivalry," and posts the quote, its citation, and her analysis (2). Later, when Samuel Hamilton's reading of Cain and Abel sparks further discussion on that moment, Millie and her classmates post their ideas in the earlier rows where the moment happened, not where the discussion happened. Additionally, one of her classmates posts a good point about Adam's name etymology, and Millie upvotes it and comments on it (3). Even when Millie falls behind, the later rows stay hidden, so she can still post on what she has read without having what she has not read spoiled (4). Finally, when Millie chooses to write about the question of free will, she filters the chart to the columns "free will," "sibling rivalry," and "Cain and Abel," and exports every idea from the whole class, in chronological order of the book and with its quote and citation (5). Thus, she no longer spends an evening processing pages of notes.
